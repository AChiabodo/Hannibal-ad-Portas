# Territory is inferred from settlements, and Attrition is a share, not a tax

**Amended by [ADR-0030](0030-settlements-occupy-ground-and-show-their-works.md),
2026-10-25, for the agreed redesign.** Quarters cover the interior of an owned
Stronghold's Walls or the inhabited core of an owned Minor settlement, replacing
the centre-Tile-only condition below. Productive exterior grounds alone do not
provide shelter; Castrum keeps its function. This is not yet implemented and does
not itself change Territory inference or seasonal Attrition percentages.

A Faction's **Territory** is the ground within `TERRITORY_RADIUS` (twelve tiles, Chebyshev) of the settlements it holds, and nothing is stored: the grid is inferred from who owns what, so land changes hands on the tick a settlement does (ADR-0005 settles supply as Territory; this ADR settles what that means on the grid). Where two Factions' squares overlap, the settlement standing nearer a tile claims it, and a tile exactly between two goes to the one the economy holds first — the Strongholds, Rome's before Carthage's. **Attrition** is charged at every Season boundary as a **share of the men a Squad is counted as**, never less than one man: `ATTRITION_PERCENT` (2) for a Season outside the Territory, `ATTRITION_WINTER_PERCENT` (6) for a winter outside it, and `ATTRITION_FIELD_WINTER_PERCENT` (2) for wintering in the open *inside* it. A Squad is in **quarters** when it is garrisoned in one of its Faction's settlements, standing on its centre, or holding position in the ground round a settlement that has a standing **Castrum** — which is Rome's alone, because `content/buildings.ts` gives the building to Rome and `applyBuild` refuses another Faction's building, and `canCamp` is that predicate read off the Faction rather than off the ground.

**Considered Options**: a fixed number of men a Season (one, or three in winter) — rejected because it is invisible in a line of a hundred and twenty men and ruinous to an elephant of twenty-four, and because it never wears a force out: attrition is meant to make a long campaign unsustainable, and only a share compounds. A drawn boundary stored beside the settlements — rejected because it is a second source of truth that drifts from the settlements it is supposed to follow, and because the map has no surveyor. A Euclidean reach, or a radius per settlement tier — rejected because Chebyshev is the whole of the map's other reaches and integer arithmetic is what state and the hash can hold (ADR-0006); a settled, tunable radius is F1's question and this one number is where it is answered. Attrition as a morale drain — rejected because C3 already charges morale for being cut off and ADR-0015 makes a fight move **two** quantities: being outside your country costs men, and being cut off costs nerve, and one boundary charges both rather than one number twice. A field camp a Squad lays down in the open, as ADR-0008 lets the ram be laid down at the walls — rejected for v1 because it needs a Command, a structure on the map and its rendering, and the Castrum the content table already carries gives Rome the same asymmetry for the price of one predicate. A camp that goes with the ground it stands on — rejected because nothing is razed on capture (ADR-0018): the first Roman village Carthage takes would hand Hannibal the field shelter the plan's asymmetry denies him, which is a rule leaking away through a single storm.

**Consequences**: `combat.ts`'s `inSupply` asks `territory.ts` instead of measuring a radius of thirty-two tiles, so `SUPPLY_RADIUS` is gone and a Squad's nerve and its men are cut off by one boundary rather than two. Territory's inference and its per-Faction view are **derived** and remembered, never state: a replay that computed them a different number of times computes the same numbers, a capture redraws the boundary through the settlement that changed hands and nothing else, and the remembered view is dropped with the grid it was made from, so no position can read another's country. `territoryView` is cached in the first place because the driver asks for an Observation on every tick, and a walk of nine thousand tiles per Faction per tick is a price an Observation may not charge; a camp is a predicate over the works a settlement holds and is asked afresh. `canCamp` is Rome's alone, and reads the Faction rather than the ground, so a captured Castrum stays a Roman camp standing in somebody else's village — nothing is razed on capture (ADR-0018's own rule), and Carthage cannot winter in it. Attrition's three percentages, `TERRITORY_RADIUS` and `CAMP_RADIUS` are the second thing F1's balance pass measures, and Carthage's inability to shelter an army in the field is the plan's asymmetry made a rule rather than a statistic. The `blockade` golden hash moved when C6 landed — Rome's besieging army now winters outside its own country — and ADR-0006 says a moved hash is a decision in review, which that was.
