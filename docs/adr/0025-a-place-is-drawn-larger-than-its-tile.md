# A settlement is drawn larger than its tile, under the fog, and picked by its drawing

A settlement stands on **one tile** of the simulation — B1's `Site`, and the place a Command names — and it is **drawn** at two tiles across for a minor settlement and three and a half for a Stronghold, centred on that tile. The rectangle it is drawn in is the rectangle a click picks: `render/settlements.ts`'s `siteRect` is handed to the settlement layer and to `input/selection.ts` alike, so a structure cannot be drawn in one place and picked in another. That is the same liberty `render/squads.ts` already takes with a block of men, which is a hundred and twenty soldiers standing up out of a single tile, and it changes nothing the rules can see: no footprint in the simulation moves, no route is priced differently, and `sim/` cannot reach this file.

**Where it stands in the stack is a decision, not a detail.** The world sorts its children by `zIndex`: the theatre at 0, the settlements on it at 1, the fog at 2, the dead at 10 and the living at 11. A place is *ground* — the veil over remembered ground has to fall on a town exactly as it falls on the field around it, which is why a remembered Stronghold is drawn as a stone silhouette under the fog rather than as a dimmed copy of its last frame above it. And a place is under the **men**: a Squad standing in a town must not be hidden behind its walls, so a Squad drawn over a place is a Squad picked before one, which is why `Pickable` carries the layer it is drawn in and not only the row it stands on.

**A remembered place is a place, and no claim about it.** `ui/sites.ts` reads what a Faction may see from three things: the theatre's own list of places (`siteIdentities`, the same geography the minimap walks), the Observation (own settlements, contacts, and the fronts it holds, besieges or has an eye on — ADR-0020), and the fog the theatre is veiled with. A place remembered is drawn with **no owner's cloth, no gate, no damage and no ram**, because a contact holds a place and not its walls; the drawing is re-tinted into the stone ramp and the fog dims it with the ground. A wall's condition comes from `sim/siege.ts`'s own hit points and from nothing else, so the drawing cannot disagree with the mechanic about what "damaged" means.

**Considered Options**: baking the settlements into the theatre texture (`render/terrain.ts`) — rejected because a settlement's appearance is a function of live state (owner, wall hit points, breach, ram, memory), and the theatre is composed once per page; a one-tile sprite drawn where the settlement stands — rejected because at the lowest supported zoom it is the size of a resource marker and a Stronghold is not a marker; picking by the tile the settlement stands on — rejected because that is not what a Player sees: a click on a tower two tiles from the centre would miss, and a structure would be unclickable in most of its own drawing; drawing the settlements above the fog, with the memory dimming painted into the sprite — rejected because the veil is one blend and a second one written into the art would drift from it; picking with a second, tile-keyed table beside the drawing — rejected because two tables are two answers to "what did the Player click", and they would eventually disagree.

**Consequences**: a place's drawing is a cached sprite per *look* (`siteLookKey`: kind, owner, memory, gate side, gate, four walls, ram), so the layer uploads nothing while nothing changes, and two towns in the same state share one texture. The breach of a wall is drawn as **two gaps either side of its middle** rather than one in the middle, because the middle of a wall is where its gatehouse stands and a single gap there would be hidden behind the gate. A Player can select a settlement the Faction does not hold — an enemy Stronghold in sight, a village it remembers, a village nobody holds — so the Selection now names places that are not the Faction's own, its panel reads only what the Observation and the fog allow (`ui/hud.ts`'s `knownSiteLines`), and the human Controller no longer prunes a settlement from the selection: a place does not leave the field, and whether the panel may still describe it is the interface's question, not the Controller's. `sites()` and `showSettlements()` join the debug hooks for the same reason `squads()` and `showSquads()` exist: a test that compares the theatre pixel for pixel has to be able to take a town out of the picture.
