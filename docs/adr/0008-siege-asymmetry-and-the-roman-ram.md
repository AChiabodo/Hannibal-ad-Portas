# Siege has two paths, and the ram is Roman

A Stronghold falls either to an **Assault** — fast, expensive, and requiring the ram, which only Rome can build — or to a **Blockade**, which is slow and cheap in lives, cuts supply, starves the settlement into surrender, and can be broken by the besieged Faction's field army. Carthage has no siege engine and therefore cannot assault at all.

**Considered Options**: both Factions able to assault, with the asymmetry carried by unit statistics — rejected because it makes the Factions differ only in numbers, which is the least interesting kind of asymmetry and the hardest to keep balanced. Assault as the only path — rejected because it turns every Match ending into the same procedure.

**Consequences**: the asymmetry is the historical one — Hannibal could not take the fortified towns of Italy — and it gives the AI two genuinely different strategies instead of one procedure. This is the plan's single largest balance risk: if Blockade is too slow, Carthage cannot win. It is measured first in the balance tournament, and the escape hatch is decided in advance: give Carthage a ram.
