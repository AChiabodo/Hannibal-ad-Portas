# An open settlement is sighted through the fog, like every other thing

A Faction's **Observation** carries a settlement's **front** — the path of its Siege, its breach, its ram, the garrison on its walls and the progress of a hold — only when the rules can say the Faction has an eye on it: it owns the settlement, it is the one besieging it, or one of its own Squads or settlements covers it. That one predicate applies to every settlement, walled or open (ADR-0013: the fog is memory and each Faction owns its own). A minor settlement has no wall, and that is what a hold takes and an assault refuses (ADR-0008); it is not a licence to skip the fog.

The earlier rule made every un-walled front visible to both Factions on the theory that an open settlement has no wall to keep a secret. It was removed in D4, when the Siege Match grew the per-Faction fog. The walk that refreshes vision and writes the last-known contacts (`refreshSiegeFog`) refuses a settlement its eyes did not reach, so a fronts filter that let an unseen village through would have put knowledge inside an Observation that the fog beside it never recorded — two answers to one question, and the looser one a free sensing channel the rules never granted. The fronts a Faction reads and the contacts it remembers are now the same sighting, made once, under `owner || besieger || eye`, written once in `observeSiege`.

**Considered Options**: keeping open settlements freely visible as an exception to vision — rejected because it grants knowledge by terrain rather than by an eye, and the Observation seam (D1) admits nothing a Faction's own eyes did not reach — a rule D5 restates for Difficulty, which never grants vision. Keeping the exception but restricting it to the garrison count — rejected for the same reason at a smaller size; which Squads a Faction keeps, and where its hold stands, is exactly what an enemy must scout. A second, cheaper rule shape for open ground — rejected because a rule with two shapes drifts, and the fog already answers the question with one predicate. Restoring the old rule outright — rejected because it would contradict the fog the Match already holds and hashes, and would make the projection disagree with the contacts memory beside it.

**Consequences**: the Siege and AI golden hashes moved when D4 landed — the fog is part of the Siege world's state and so of its hash, and an Observation is now strictly a subset of what the fog recorded. `tests/sim/siege.test.ts` pins the rule: an open minor settlement outside every eye is absent from an Observation, and a watcher mustered within vision brings it in; the sibling test that leaves a settlement open had to muster that watcher when the rule changed, which is the change made visible. Finding an unseen frontier is the scouting policy's job (D4), and nothing in the rules announces one.
